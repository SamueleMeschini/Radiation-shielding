# 25-minute guided sequence

- **0–4 min: Predict.** Ask students whether lead, concrete, or water gives the smallest photon-number transmission at equal centimetres. Select 661.657 keV and compare at a common physical thickness. Reveal only after a vote.
- **4–8 min: Change the basis.** Switch to equal areal density, then equal optical thickness. Ask why the ranking can change; distinguish μ from μ/ρ and τ. Identify the exact uncollided curve and uncertainty bars.
- **8–12 min: Scattering and energy.** Inspect Bₙ and Bₑ, and the spectrum at a direct point. Ask why total current can exceed uncollided current and why the mean outgoing energy can differ from source energy. Emphasize that spectrum bins cannot identify a unique mechanism.
- **12–16 min: Interactions.** Change photon energy and material. Discuss first-interaction probabilities and the 1.022 MeV pair threshold. Sweep the Compton angle from 0° to 180° and derive the backscatter limit.
- **16–20 min: Mixed source.** Select equal 0.100/1.000 MeV photons. Follow the high-energy share and distinguish incremental from cumulative attenuation. Ask which part of the source is removed first in the selected material.
- **20–23 min: Layer order.** Compare Pb–Sn–Cu with Cu–Sn–Pb only where direct stack runs are available. Why does the uncollided expression ignore order while the transmitted spectrum may not? Do not attribute a peak to fluorescence without supporting tally or spectroscopy evidence.
- **23–25 min: Challenge.** Reveal the minimum areal-density point for a selected transmission target. Ask what additional simulation points and uncertainty improvements would be needed before using it beyond class. No dose calculation is involved.

The charged-particle lab can be used as a brief extension: discuss why low-Z material is normally placed first for beta shielding, and how the local ESTAR/ASTAR stopping-power curves lead to mass and physical CSDA ranges.
